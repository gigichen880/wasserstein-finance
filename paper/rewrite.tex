\begin{abstract}
Population snapshots can predict one observed population while leaving the
decomposition of its energy ambiguous. We exhibit a gauge that transfers a
quadratic form between external confinement and pairwise interaction. Subject
to admissibility and uniqueness, the transfer is invisible along any
population path whose mean lies in the gauge null space, even when the base
energy is nonlinear. In a scalar quadratic model we then separate three
questions. First, centered marginal laws depend on confinement and interaction
only through their sum, while retaining dependence on diffusion; they need not
identify both the combined rate and diffusion. All points on the
confinement--interaction ridge therefore share the same centered laws. Second,
near that ridge, identification is controlled by two independent
excitation directions. Information for the confinement rate is exactly
quadratic in the initial mean, while information for the variance rate
vanishes quadratically as the initial variance approaches equilibrium.
We give exact Gaussian Kullback--Leibler divergences, nuisance-adjusted
information, local expansions of the profiled Fisher matrix, and
finite-sample two-point lower bounds for both mechanisms. Maximizing
information for the confinement rate alone can destroy joint identifiability
of the energy decomposition.
Third, the ambiguity is consequential: ridge-equivalent models disagree after
a physical shift of the initial population. Under equal sampling budgets,
training on a shifted population recovers the decomposition and sharply
contracts forecast error on an independent shifted population, whereas
centered training retains a set of intervention forecasts. Weak-variance
interval constructions are reported only as supporting diagnostics.
The recovery
guarantees are for the proved quadratic model; a shifted population reveals
the displayed gauge but does not establish general nonlinear identifiability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many learning problems recover the dynamics of a population from
\emph{snapshots}. At a few times one observes an unlabeled sample from the
current distribution of states, and never the trajectory of an individual.
Single-cell measurements are the standard example~\cite{Schiebinger19}. A
common structural assumption is that the population follows a Wasserstein
gradient flow
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gf}
\partial_t\rho_t
=
\nabla\cdot\Bigl(\rho_t\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\rho}(\rho_t)\Bigr)
\end{equation}
of an unknown free energy $F$~\cite{JKO98,AGS08}. Several methods learn $F$
from snapshots by differentiating through its Jordan--Kinderlehrer--Otto (JKO)
discretization or its first-order optimality condition, and they typically
parametrize a potential, a pairwise interaction, and an entropy term
jointly~\cite{Bunne22,Terpin24}. Those procedures can match an observed
sequence of marginals while leaving unidentified which component of $F$
generated the flow.

For non-interacting gradient-flow diffusions, the drift and diffusivity are
identifiable from the marginal flow if and only if the process is observed out
of equilibrium~\cite{Guan26}. Interaction changes the picture. We give an exact
account for the quadratic free energy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F}
F(\rho)
=
\frac a2\int x^2\rho(x)\,dx
+
\frac b2\Var(\rho)
+
\beta\int\rho\log\rho\,dx,
\qquad a>0,\; b\ge 0,\; \beta>0,
\end{equation}
on $\Ptwo(\R)$. The interaction term is the energy of the kernel
$W(z)=\frac b2 z^2$. Its gradient flow is a quadratic mean-field Langevin
dynamics. The gradient-flow structure and the contraction are
classical~\cite{McCann97,Malrieu03,CarrilloMcCannVillani03,CarrilloMcCannVillani06};
the learning statements below use the explicit affine law.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Exact decomposition ambiguity.} Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge}
gives a potential--interaction gauge for general base energies. In the scalar
quadratic model, centered populations generate an exact ridge
$(a,b,\beta)\mapsto(a+h,b-h,\beta)$.
\item \emph{Weak identification in two distinct directions.}
Propositions~\ref{prop:weak-mean} and~\ref{prop:weak-var} give exact KL
comparisons, nuisance-adjusted information, and Le Cam bounds. Small mean
excitation weakens separation of confinement from interaction; near-equilibrium
variance separately weakens recovery of dispersion dynamics.
\item \emph{Excitation geometry and joint snapshot design.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry} records that $I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is exactly
quadratic in $\mu_0$, that $I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is locally quadratic in
$\Sigma_0-q$, and that $\lambda_{\min}$ of the profiled Fisher for
$(a,b,\beta)$ collapses along the corresponding kernels. Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-design}
states that maximizing information for $a$ alone can destroy identifiability
of $(k,q)$.
\item \emph{Intervention disagreement and recovery.} Ridge-equivalent models
agree on centered training laws but disagree on a physically shifted
population. Equal-budget experiments compare centered-only, shifted-only, and
mixed initializations on an independent shifted test population. The centered
fit is retained as a set, not reduced to an arbitrary optimizer output.
\item \emph{Regular estimation away from the weak sets.} The exact moment map
yields consistent and efficient estimators (Theorems~\ref{thm:est}
and~\ref{thm:lam}) when the mean moves and the variance is not already at
equilibrium.
\item \emph{Supporting checks.} Interval procedures under weak variance
excitation are diagnosed rather than treated as a main recovery method.
Optimal-transport alignment is blind to uniform time rescaling
(Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}). Forward-solver convergence and JKO structure
are reported in the appendix.
\end{enumerate}

One coordinate reading of $x$ is a dealer's signed inventory, with
$\frac a2 x^2$ a position penalty~\cite{AvellanedaStoikov08,GueantLehalleFernandez2013}.
This is a stylized myopic gradient flow, not a finite-horizon market-making
game~\cite{LasryLions07,BaldacciBergaultPossamai2023}; see
Appendix~\ref{app:background}. Nothing below is a test on observed inventories.

\paragraph{Related work.}
The variational form of the Fokker--Planck equation is due to~\citet{JKO98}. The
metric theory is that of~\citet{AGS08}, following the Otto calculus~\cite{Otto01} and
displacement convexity~\cite{McCann97}; surveys are~\cite{Villani03,Santambrogio15}.
Contraction for McKean--Vlasov equations with convex confinement and interaction
is developed by~\citet{CarrilloMcCannVillani03,CarrilloMcCannVillani06,Malrieu03}.
JKOnet learns a potential energy from snapshots~\cite{Bunne22}. JKOnet*
fits potential, interaction, and internal energy jointly and gives a unique
linear-dictionary solution when a stacked Gram matrix is
invertible~\cite{Terpin24}. iJKOnet treats temporally unpaired snapshots, with
a recovery theorem for potential-only models~\cite{Persiianov26}. Identifiability
for a single gradient-flow drift and unknown diffusion is characterized
by~\citet{Guan26}; that result can identify a combined McKean--Vlasov drift
without splitting confinement from interaction. Invisible drift directions and
weak snapshot estimation for one drift field with known diffusion are studied
by~\citet{NguyenMalysheva26}. Identifiability of interaction kernels with a
known external potential and a full density path is developed
by~\citet{LangLu22,LangLu23}. Trajectory-based inference for interacting
particles is a different observation model~\cite{Sznitman91}. Our target is
narrower: a component-decomposition gauge inside an interacting energy,
statistical deterioration near that gauge, and population-level interventions
that remove it in the quadratic model.

The Gaussian affine law is a microscope, not the claim. The claim is that a
method which jointly parametrizes a potential, an interaction, and an entropy
from unpaired snapshots can reproduce an observed marginal flow while leaving
the energy components unidentified. Non-interacting identifiability~\cite{Guan26}
recovers one drift field; kernel recovery with a known
potential~\cite{LangLu22,LangLu23} never has to split $V$ from $W$. The
quadratic model makes the remaining obstruction, its two weak-identification
directions, and the excitation that removes it completely explicit. The
calculations that follow are elementary precisely because that is what an
exact characterization requires.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

$\Ptwo(\R^d)$ is the set of Borel probabilities with finite second moment,
metrized by
$\Wtwo^2(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int|x-y|^2\,d\gamma$.
Appendix~\ref{app:background} recalls dynamic optimal transport, Brenier maps,
and the mean-field-game system. For a confining potential $V$, a symmetric
interaction $W$, and $\beta>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F-general}
F(\rho)
=
\int V\rho\,dx
+
\frac12\iint W(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy
+
\beta\int\rho\log\rho\,dx,
\end{equation}
with first variation
$\delta F/\delta\rho=V+W\ast\rho+\beta(\log\rho+1)$.
A curve is a $W_2$ gradient flow of $F$ when it solves~\eqref{eq:gf}.
Substituting the first variation produces the McKean--Vlasov equation
$\partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla V)+\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla(W\ast\rho))+\beta\Delta\rho$,
and the energy dissipates at rate
$\frac{d}{dt}F(\rho_t)=-\int\rho_t\bigl|\nabla\delta F/\delta\rho\bigr|^2\,dx\le 0$.
The JKO scheme with step $\tau>0$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jko}
\rho_{k+1}
\in
\arg\min_{\rho\in\Ptwo}
\Bigl\{
F(\rho)+\frac1{2\tau}\Wtwo^2(\rho,\rho_k)
\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}

Throughout, $d=1$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quad}
V(x)=\frac a2 x^2,
\qquad
W(z)=\frac b2 z^2,
\qquad
a>0,\; b\ge 0,\; \beta>0.
\end{equation}
Then $\frac12\iint W\rho\rho=\frac b2\Var(\rho)$, and $F$ is~\eqref{eq:F}.
We write $\theta=(a,b,\beta)$, $k=a+b$, and
$q=\sigma_\infty^2=\beta/k$. The symbols $q$ and $\sigma_\infty^2$ are
interchangeable; information calculations use $q$.
Since $\nabla(W\ast\rho)(x)=b(x-\mu(\rho))$, the interaction is a penalty on
cross-sectional dispersion.

\section{A potential--interaction gauge}
\label{sec:gauge}

The central ambiguity is not restricted to a purely quadratic energy. For
$m(\rho)=\int x\,d\rho(x)$ and a symmetric matrix $H$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gauge}
V_H(x)=V(x)+\frac12x^\top Hx,
\qquad
W_H(z)=W(z)-\frac12z^\top Hz.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic transfer gauge]
\label{prop:gauge}
Write $F_{V,W,\beta}$ for~\eqref{eq:F-general} and let
\[
\operatorname{dom} F
=
\{\rho\in\Ptwo(\R^d):
F_{V,W,\beta}(\rho),\;
F_{V_H,W_H,\beta}(\rho)\in\mathbb R\}.
\]
The component integrals in~\eqref{eq:F-general} are required to be well-defined
real numbers, not an indeterminate $\infty-\infty$. In particular
$F(\rho)<\infty$ does not exclude $F(\rho)=-\infty$, and a finite second moment
does not by itself make either energy real-valued.
On this common real-valued domain the quadratic-transfer identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gauge-energy}
F_{V_H,W_H,\beta}(\rho)-F_{V,W,\beta}(\rho)
=\frac12m(\rho)^\top Hm(\rho)
\end{equation}
holds, so both sides are real. If $V$ and $W$ are differentiable,
$\nabla V$ and $\nabla V_H$ exist $\rho$-almost everywhere, and the
interaction-gradient convolutions $\nabla W*\rho$ and $\nabla W_H*\rho$ exist
as absolutely convergent integrals $\rho$-almost everywhere, the force identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gauge-force}
\nabla V_H+\nabla W_H*\rho-(\nabla V+\nabla W*\rho)
=Hm(\rho)
\end{equation}
holds $\rho$-almost everywhere. Local integrability of the first variations
does not by itself give those absolutely convergent convolutions. Suppose both
transformed and untransformed energies belong to the declared admissible class,
their equations exist and are unique from a common initial law, and a solution
satisfies $Hm_t=0$ for $t\in[0,T]$. Then that path solves both equations, so
all of its marginals coincide under the two decompositions.
\end{proposition}

This proposition is a sufficient ambiguity mechanism, not a classification of
all ambiguities. Admissibility does real work: the transfer must preserve the
growth, coercivity, convexity, or other hypotheses used for well-posedness. In
the scalar quadratic model it becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scalar-ridge}
(a,b,\beta)\longmapsto(a+h,b-h,\beta),
\qquad a+h>0,\quad b-h\ge0.
\end{equation}
An even initial law remains centered under an even, unique flow, so the same
transfer can be invisible on top of nonlinear even confinement and interaction
terms. We do not claim that this exhausts nonlinear non-identifiability.

\section{Exact structure}
\label{sec:structure}

Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{proposition}[Gradient-flow form]
\label{prop:gf}
The $W_2$ gradient flow of~\eqref{eq:F} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mv}
\partial_t m_t
=
\partial_x\bigl(m_t[kx-b\mu_t]\bigr)
+
\beta\,\partial_{xx}m_t,
\qquad
\mu_t=\int x\,m_t(dx).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\partial_t m_t=-\partial_x(v_t m_t)$ with Otto velocity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:velocity}
v_t(x)=-kx+b\mu_t-\beta\,\partial_x\log m_t(x).
\end{equation}
Within the class of finite-energy, absolutely continuous stationary
solutions, the equilibria are the McKean--Vlasov--Gibbs measures
$m_\infty\propto\exp\bigl(-\beta^{-1}(V+W\ast m_\infty)\bigr)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Equilibrium, exact law, and contraction]
\label{thm:gauss}
Let $m_0\in\Ptwo(\R)$ have mean $\mu_0$ and variance $\Sigma_0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The unique equilibrium is $m_\infty=N(0,\sigma_\infty^2)$ with
$\sigma_\infty^2=\beta/(a+b)$.
\item[(ii)] The solution is the law of
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:affine}
X_t
=
\mu_0 e^{-at}
+
e^{-kt}(X_0-\mu_0)
+
\sqrt{\sigma_\infty^2(1-e^{-2kt})}\,Z,
\end{equation}
where $X_0\sim m_0$ and $Z\sim N(0,1)$ are independent. In particular
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:moments}
\mu_t=\mu_0 e^{-at},
\qquad
\Sigma_t
=
\sigma_\infty^2+(\Sigma_0-\sigma_\infty^2)e^{-2kt}.
\end{equation}
Whenever $m_0$ has a finite cumulant of order $j\ge 3$,
$\kappa_j(m_t)=e^{-jkt}\kappa_j(m_0)$.
\item[(iii)] $F$ is $a$-geodesically convex on $(\Ptwo(\R),\Wtwo)$, so
$\Wtwo(m_t,m_\infty)\le e^{-at}\Wtwo(m_0,m_\infty)$, and the JKO iterates satisfy
$\Wtwo(\rho_k,m_\infty)\le(1+a\tau)^{-k}\Wtwo(\rho_0,m_\infty)$.
If $\mu_0\neq 0$, the rate $a$ is attained by the mean and is not improved by
$b$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Energy dissipation of the proximal map~\eqref{eq:jko} and its convergence to
this flow are recorded as Theorem~\ref{thm:jko} in
Appendix~\ref{app:jko1d}.

The equilibrium dispersion $\beta/(a+b)$ decreases in the interaction strength.
The mean is the slow mode. A piecewise-constant tilt
$V_t(x)=\frac a2(x-c)^2$ on an interval of length $\Delta T$ moves the mean by
the exact rule $\mu(t_0+\Delta T)=c+(\mu(t_0)-c)e^{-a\Delta T}$. That identity is
the liquidity-shock check in Appendix~\ref{app:experiments}. On a forcing
interval the untilted energy need not decrease.

\section{Learning from snapshots}
\label{sec:learning}

Fix times $t_j=j\Delta$, $j=0,1,2$, with $\Delta>0$. At time $t_j$ we observe
$N$ i.i.d.\ samples from $m_{t_j}$, independent across $j$. No sample is linked
across time. Write $(\hat\mu_j,\hat\Sigma_j)$ for the sample mean and unbiased
sample variance, and $(\mu_j,\Sigma_j)$ for the population values.
Appendix experiments that evolve one cloud of interacting particles are a
different design; see Remark~\ref{rem:particles}.

\subsection{Identifiability}

\begin{proposition}[Identifiability from the marginal flow]
\label{prop:ident}
Let $m^\theta$ solve~\eqref{eq:mv} with parameter $\theta$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $\mu_0=0$, then $m^\theta_t$ depends on $\theta$ only through
$(a+b,\beta)$.
\item[(ii)] If $m_0=N(\mu_0,\beta/(a+b))$, then
$m^\theta_t=N(\mu_0 e^{-at},\beta/(a+b))$ depends on $\theta$ only through
$(a,\beta/(a+b))$.
\item[(iii)] If $\mu_0\neq 0$ and $\Sigma_0\neq\beta/(a+b)$, the triple
$(\mu_j,\Sigma_j)_{j=0,1,2}$ determines $\theta$ for every $\Delta>0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:g}
\begin{gathered}
a=-\frac1\Delta\log\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_0},
\qquad
k=-\frac1{2\Delta}\log r,
\qquad
\sigma_\infty^2
=
\Sigma_0-\frac{\Sigma_1-\Sigma_0}{r-1},\\
b=k-a,
\qquad
\beta=k\sigma_\infty^2,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $r=(\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1)/(\Sigma_1-\Sigma_0)=e^{-2k\Delta}\in(0,1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The two exclusions are sharp for moment data. A centered population feels
$b(x-\mu_t)$ only as additional confinement, so snapshots taken far from
equilibrium still cannot separate $a$ from $b$. A centered law therefore
depends only on $(a+b,\beta)$, but need not identify both coordinates: a
centered equilibrium Gaussian identifies only $q=\beta/(a+b)$. A Gaussian
started at the equilibrium variance never moves its dispersion, so the
variance path carries no information about $k$. If $\Sigma_0=\sigma_\infty^2$
but $m_0$ is not Gaussian, Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss}(ii) identifies $k$ from a
higher cumulant only when some finite, nonzero cumulant of order $j\ge 3$ is
available. Any method that jointly parametrizes $V$, $W$, and $\beta$ from
unpaired snapshots can therefore match the displayed centered marginal path
while leaving the energy split unidentified. A moving mean is necessary to
break the ridge, and is not automatically sufficient.

\subsection{Weak identification}

Exact non-identification is the endpoint of a statistical deterioration, but
two mechanisms must be kept separate. Throughout this subsection the initial
law is Gaussian,
\[
\rho_0=N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0),
\]
snapshots are independent across times, and snapshot $j$ contains $N_j$
independent observations. The displayed Kullback--Leibler formulas use these
Gaussian marginals; they are not claimed for a non-Gaussian $m_0$. Write
\[
S_j=q+(\Sigma_0-q)e^{-2kt_j},\qquad k=a+b,\quad q=\beta/k.
\]
Admissible alternatives satisfy $a>0$, $k\ge a$, and $q>0$.

\begin{proposition}[Weak mean excitation]
\label{prop:weak-mean}
Hold $(k,\beta,\Sigma_0)$ fixed and compare the admissible alternatives
$a$ and $a+\delta$, with known common initial mean $\mu_0$. The laws remain
Gaussian with a common variance path, so their exact
Kullback--Leibler divergence is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mean-kl}
K_{\mathrm{mean}}(\delta)
=\frac12\sum_j
\frac{N_j\mu_0^2
\left(e^{-at_j}-e^{-(a+\delta)t_j}\right)^2}{S_j}.
\end{equation}
If $\mu_0$ is unknown, minimizing over the alternative initial mean gives
\[
\mu_0'=\mu_0
\frac{\sum_jw_je^{-at_j}e^{-(a+\delta)t_j}}
{\sum_jw_je^{-2(a+\delta)t_j}},
\qquad w_j=N_j/S_j.
\]
The local efficient information after projecting out $\mu_0$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mean-info}
I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}
=\mu_0^2\sum_j
\frac{N_je^{-2at_j}}{S_j}(t_j-\bar t)^2,
\quad
\bar t=
\frac{\sum_jN_je^{-2at_j}t_j/S_j}
{\sum_jN_je^{-2at_j}/S_j}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, under a fixed nondegenerate design,
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ scales as $N\mu_0^2$.
\end{proposition}

Thus a fixed nonzero mean gives ordinary root-$N$ information,
$\mu_{0,N}\asymp N^{-1/2}$ leaves bounded information, and $\mu_0=0$
produces the exact ridge~\eqref{eq:scalar-ridge}. At least two distinct times
are needed when the initial mean is unknown.

\begin{proposition}[Weak variance excitation]
\label{prop:weak-var}
Hold $(a,\mu_0,\Sigma_0,q)$ fixed and compare alternatives $k$ and $k+\delta$,
both required to remain admissible, so $k\ge a$ and $k+\delta\ge a$. Then the
Gaussian means coincide, $b=k-a$, $\beta=kq$, and $\varepsilon=\Sigma_0-q$. The
exact KL is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:var-kl}
K_{\mathrm{var}}(\delta)
=\frac12\sum_jN_j
\left[
\frac{S_j(k)}{S_j(k+\delta)}
-1-\log\frac{S_j(k)}{S_j(k+\delta)}
\right].
\end{equation}
For nuisance parameters $(\Sigma_0,q)$, put $r_j=e^{-2kt_j}$ and write
\[
\langle u,v\rangle_S=\sum_j N_j u_j v_j/(2S_j^2).
\]
The efficient information is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:var-info}
I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}
=\left\|
(I-\Pi_{\operatorname{span}\{r,1-r\}})
(-2\varepsilon\,tr)
\right\|_S^2
=
\varepsilon^2 C(\varepsilon;k,q,\{t_j,N_j\}).
\end{equation}
The weights $S_j=q+\varepsilon e^{-2kt_j}$ depend on $\varepsilon$, so $C$ is not a
universal constant. Under a design for which
$\{r,1-r,-2tr\}$ has rank three in the $S$-inner product after sending
$\varepsilon\to 0$, one has $C(\varepsilon)\to C(0)>0$. Three distinct times with
positive allocations are sufficient for that rank. With only two distinct
times and both $(\Sigma_0,q)$ unknown, $\{r,1-r\}$ already spans the
two-dimensional variance tangent, so $I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=0$ even away from
equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

The candidate scaling $N(\Sigma_0-q)^2$ is therefore a local description after
the design, variance scale, and nuisance geometry have been fixed. It is not a
universal collapse coordinate.

The two scalar formulas are the scores of the affine law. Because
$\mu_t=\mu_0 e^{-at}$ one has $\partial_a\mu_t=-t\mu_0 e^{-at}$, so the mean
score is linear in $\mu_0$ and the information is exactly quadratic in
$\mu_0$. Because $\Sigma_t=q+\varepsilon e^{-2kt}$ one has
$\partial_k\Sigma_t=-2t\varepsilon e^{-2kt}$, so the variance score is linear
in $\varepsilon$. The profiled matrix for $(a,b,\beta)$ inherits those
kernels.

\begin{proposition}[Excitation geometry]
\label{prop:geometry}
Assume the Gaussian snapshot experiment of this subsection, with unknown
nuisances $(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$. Write $\varepsilon=\Sigma_0-q$ and let
$I(\mu_0,\varepsilon)$ be the Fisher information for $(a,b,\beta)$ after
projecting out $(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The path $(S_j)$ does not depend on $\mu_0$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ia-factor}
I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}
=\mu_0^2 I_a^{(1)}(a,k,q,\Sigma_0,\{t_j,N_j\})
\end{equation}
exactly, for every $\mu_0$, where $I_a^{(1)}$ is~\eqref{eq:mean-info} at
$\mu_0=1$. The factor $I_a^{(1)}$ is positive whenever at least two distinct
times have positive allocation.
\item[(ii)] $I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon^2 C(\varepsilon)$ with
$C(\varepsilon)\to C(0)$. Three distinct times with positive allocation give
$C(0)>0$. Two distinct times give $C(\varepsilon)=0$ for every $\varepsilon$.
\item[(iii)] At fixed $\varepsilon\neq 0$,
\[
\lambda_{\min}\bigl(I(\mu_0,\varepsilon)\bigr)
=\frac{\mu_0^2}{2}\,I_a^{(1)}+O(\mu_0^4)
\qquad(\mu_0\to 0),
\]
and the associated eigenspace approaches the gauge $(1,-1,0)$ in
$(a,b,\beta)$. At fixed $\mu_0\neq 0$,
$\lambda_{\min}(I(\mu_0,\varepsilon))=\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ as
$\varepsilon\to 0$, and the associated direction approaches $(0,1,q)$.
\item[(iv)] Local identifiability of $(a,b,\beta)$ therefore requires both
excitations, with enough distinct times to profile the nuisances. Vanishing
mean excitation removes information about $a$; vanishing variance excitation
removes information about $k$, hence about $b=k-a$ and $\beta=kq$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The $O(\mu_0^4)$ remainder is ordinary perturbation of a simple kernel of the
variance block: $I(\mu_0,\varepsilon)=I_{\mathrm{var}}(\varepsilon)+\mu_0^2 B$
exactly, with $B$ independent of $\mu_0$, and
$u^\top B u=I_a^{(1)}/2$ for the unit gauge $u=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt 2$.

\begin{corollary}[Joint snapshot design]
\label{cor:joint-design}
A design that maximizes $I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ need not maximize
$\lambda_{\min}(I)$. Any two-time experiment with unknown $(\Sigma_0,q)$ has
$I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=0$, hence $\lambda_{\min}(I)=0$, even if
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is large. Informative designs occupy an interior time
scale and must be scored jointly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-sample two-point difficulty]
\label{cor:lecam}
Let $K$ be a fully specified Kullback--Leibler divergence:~\eqref{eq:mean-kl}
with known common $\mu_0$, or~\eqref{eq:var-kl}. Both experiments are pairs of
product Gaussian laws with no remaining nuisance. For every estimator of the
varied rate,
\[
\max_iP_i\!\left(|\widehat\theta-\theta_i|\ge|\delta|/2\right)
\ge\frac12\left(1-\sqrt{K/2}\right),
\qquad
\max_i\mathbb E_i(\widehat\theta-\theta_i)^2
\ge\frac{\delta^2}{8}\left(1-\sqrt{K/2}\right).
\]
Allowing different nuisance initial means yields the minimized KL and a
potentially larger two-point risk lower bound, provided both alternatives
belong to the declared parameter space.
\end{corollary}

These are information-theoretic statements about the observation experiment.
They do not imply that every estimator attains the bound, nor do failures of a
particular clipped or constrained estimator strengthen the impossibility
claim.

\subsection{Intervention forecasting}

The same ridge that is invisible on a centered training population produces
distinct forecasts after a physical shift of the initial law. Recentering
stored observations is not that experiment: the energy is fixed and a new
population is initialized.

\begin{proposition}[Shifted intervention]
\label{prop:intervention}
Let $\theta_h=(a+h,b-h,\beta)$ with $k=a+b$ and $q=\beta/k$ fixed, and write
$m^{(h)}$ for the law of~\eqref{eq:affine} under $\theta_h$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $\mu_0=0$, then $m^{(h)}_t=m^{(0)}_t$ for every admissible $h$
and every $t\ge 0$.
\item[(ii)] If a physical intervention translates a centered $Y_0$ by $s$, then
\[
W_2\bigl(m^{(h)}_t,m^{(h')}_t\bigr)
=
|s|\,\bigl|e^{-(a+h)t}-e^{-(a+h')t}\bigr|.
\]
\item[(iii)] If the initial means of several populations are known design
inputs and the populations share a common variance path, the Gaussian
mean-block information for $a$ is proportional to $\sum_r N_r\mu_{0,r}^2$.
Under equal per-time budgets, a mixed design with means $\{-s,0,s\}$ therefore
has two thirds of the mean-block information of a shifted-only design with
mean $s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

A likelihood fit on centered data is a set along~\eqref{eq:scalar-ridge}, not
a recovered decomposition. Test-population data are not used to select a
ridge member. Shifted training can restore point recovery of $a$ within this
family; it does not prove complete nonlinear identifiability of arbitrary
$V$ and $W$.

\subsection{Estimation}

Let $\hat\theta_N$ be the plug-in estimator obtained by substituting
$(\hat\mu_j,\hat\Sigma_j)$ into~\eqref{eq:g}, on the event
$\{\hat\mu_1/\hat\mu_0>0,\ \hat r\in(0,1)\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Consistency and asymptotic normality]
\label{thm:est}
Assume $\int x^4\,m_0(dx)<\infty$, $\mu_0\neq 0$, and
$\Sigma_0\neq\beta/(a+b)$. Then the defining event has probability tending to
one, $\hat\theta_N\to\theta$ almost surely, and
$\sqrt N(\hat\theta_N-\theta)\Rightarrow N(0,J\Gamma J^\top)$.
Here $\Gamma$ is block diagonal across snapshots, the $(j)$-th block is the
covariance of $(X,(X-\mu_j)^2)$ under $m_{t_j}$,
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\Sigma_j & \kappa_{3,j}\\
\kappa_{3,j} & \kappa_{4,j}+2\Sigma_j^2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and $J$ is the Jacobian of~\eqref{eq:g}. The asymptotic variance of
$\sqrt N(\hat a-a)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:avar}
v_a(\Delta)
=
\frac1{\Delta^2}
\Bigl(
\frac{\Sigma_0}{\mu_0^2}
+
\frac{\Sigma_1}{\mu_0^2 e^{-2a\Delta}}
\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The estimator of $a$ uses only the means. Close snapshots have little rate
signal, through the factor $\Delta^{-2}$. Distant snapshots see a mean that has
already decayed, through $e^{2a\Delta}$. The conditions on $\Sigma_0$ are
required for $b$ and $\beta$.

\begin{corollary}[Spacing when the variance does not move]
\label{cor:spacing}
If $\Sigma_0=\Sigma_1$, then
$v_a(\Delta)=\Sigma_0(1+e^{2a\Delta})/(\mu_0^2\Delta^2)$, with unique minimizer
$\Delta^\star=x^\star/a$. The constant $x^\star\approx 1.109$ is the unique
positive solution of $x=1+e^{-2x}$.
\end{corollary}

When the variance does move,~\eqref{eq:avar} is minimized numerically from a
pilot estimate of $(a,k,\Sigma_0)$.

\paragraph{Efficiency for Gaussian initial laws.}
Consider snapshots at $t_0<\cdots<t_J$, $J\ge 1$, and the parametric model in
which $m_0=N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ with $\mu_0\neq 0$ and $\Sigma_0$ unknown.
Let $\omega_j=\mu_{t_j}^2/\Sigma_{t_j}$, and let $\bar t_\omega$ and
\[
\Var_\omega(t)
=
\frac{\sum_j\omega_j(t_j-\bar t_\omega)^2}{\sum_j\omega_j}
\]
be the $\omega$-weighted mean and variance of the snapshot times.

\begin{theorem}[Local asymptotic minimax bound]
\label{thm:lam}
Assume $b>0$ and at least two distinct observation times. In this Gaussian
model the Fisher information is block diagonal between
$(\mu_0,a)$ and $(\Sigma_0,k,q)$, equivalently
$(\Sigma_0,a+b,\sigma_\infty^2)$. For every estimator sequence
$T_N$ of $a$ and every bowl-shaped loss $\ell$,
\[
\lim_{c\to\infty}
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\sup_{\|h\|\le c}
\mathbb E_{\vartheta+h/\sqrt N}
\,\ell\bigl(\sqrt N(T_N-a_{\vartheta+h/\sqrt N})\bigr)
\ \ge\
\mathbb E\,\ell(Z),
\]
where $Z\sim N\bigl(0,\ 1/(\sum_j\omega_j\Var_\omega(t))\bigr)$ and
$\vartheta=(\mu_0,a,\Sigma_0,a+b,\sigma_\infty^2)$.
The bound is attained by weighted least squares of $\mu_0 e^{-at}$ on
$(\hat\mu_j)$, with weights $N/\hat\Sigma_j$. For two snapshots at spacing
$\Delta$ the bound equals $v_a(\Delta)$, so the plug-in $\hat a$ is
asymptotically efficient. For three snapshots and $\Sigma_0\neq\sigma_\infty^2$,
the plug-in estimators of $a+b$, $\sigma_\infty^2$, and $\beta=k\sigma_\infty^2$
are asymptotically efficient.
At $b=0$, ordinary two-sided LAN neighborhoods leave the admissible set
$b\ge 0$, so the interior efficiency statement does not apply.
\end{theorem}

The rate $a$ is learned from the decay of the mean, and only from snapshots in
which that mean is large relative to the spread. The pair $(a+b,\beta)$ is
learned from the relaxation of the variance. Section~\ref{sec:numerics} checks
the bound on two and three snapshots.

\begin{remark}[One interacting system]
\label{rem:particles}
If every snapshot is the empirical measure of a single system of $N$ particles
started i.i.d.\ from $m_0$ with a finite fourth moment, write
$\widetilde B_t=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N B^i_t$ for the standard Brownian motion
obtained from the driving noises. The linear interaction gives the exact
empirical-mean SDE
\[
d\bar X_t=-a\bar X_t\,dt+\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{N}}\,d\widetilde B_t.
\]
Hence $\bar X_t-\mu_t=O_p(N^{-1/2})$ uniformly on compact time intervals, by
the explicit Ornstein--Uhlenbeck representation of that linear SDE. Centered
deviations $Y^i_t=X^i_t-\bar X_t$ satisfy
\[
dY^i_t=-k Y^i_t\,dt+\sqrt{2\beta}\Bigl(dB^i_t-\frac1{\sqrt N}\,d\widetilde B_t\Bigr).
\]
The empirical second moment of $(Y^i_t)$ is likewise $O_p(N^{-1/2})$ about
$\Sigma_t$ at each $t$. This rate is for the linear particle system; it is not
quoted from a generic propagation-of-chaos bound. A plug-in estimator of
$\theta$ remains $\sqrt N$-consistent on this design when $\mu_0\neq 0$ and
$\Sigma_0\neq\sigma_\infty^2$, but dependence across particles and across times
changes the asymptotic covariance, and Theorems~\ref{thm:est}
and~\ref{thm:lam} do not apply as stated. The particle experiments in
Appendix~\ref{app:experiments} use that design.
\end{remark}

Alignment of the monotone rearrangement with the Wasserstein velocity is an
interpretability check after the time scale is fixed; it is stated as
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} in Appendix~\ref{app:alignment}.

\section{Numerical verification}
\label{sec:numerics}

Unless noted, $a=1$, $b=\tfrac12$, $\beta=\tfrac12$, so $q=\sigma_\infty^2=1/3$,
the mean rate is $1$, and the variance rate is $3$. Laws in this section are
independent snapshots from~\eqref{eq:affine}. Solver, relaxation, alignment,
and particle-system checks are in Appendix~\ref{app:experiments}; they are
not identification evidence. Throughout we keep three statements distinct:
population-level impossibility, finite-information lower bounds, and the
behavior of a particular estimator or interval.

\paragraph{Well-excited estimation.}
The initial law
$m_0=\tfrac12 N(-1.2,0.2^2)+\tfrac12 N(2.8,0.2^2)$ has $\mu_0=0.8$ and
$\Sigma_0=4.04$. Three independent snapshots are drawn from~\eqref{eq:affine}
and passed to the plug-in map of Theorem~\ref{thm:est}, over $2000$
replications. On the valid plug-in event of Theorem~\ref{thm:est},
root-mean-square error of $(\hat a,\hat b,\hat\beta)$ decays as
$N^{-1/2}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:est}a). At this spacing the valid-event rate is
$100\%$ across the simulated replications at every displayed $N$, so the RMSE
is not a selected subset. At $N=2000$, the Monte Carlo value of
$N\Var(\hat a)$ tracks~\eqref{eq:avar}: $46.4$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,1.5$) against
$45.0$ at $\Delta=0.5$,
and $8.05$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,0.25$) against $8.03$ at $\Delta=1.5$ (Figure~\ref{fig:est}b). Because the
variance falls from $4.04$ toward $1/3$, the minimizer of~\eqref{eq:avar} on
this path is $\Delta\approx 1.62$, rather than the constant-variance value
$1.109$.

For the Gaussian efficiency check, $m_0=N(0.8,1.5)$ and $\Delta=0.75$, again
with $N=2000$ and $2000$ replications. The two-snapshot plug-in has
$N\Var(\hat a)=9.53$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,0.30$) against the bound $9.85$. Weighted least squares on the
means at $0,\Delta,2\Delta$ has $4.44$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,0.14$) against the bound $4.63$.
The corresponding RMSE and spacing plots are Figure~\ref{fig:est} in
Appendix~\ref{app:experiments}.

\paragraph{Centered ambiguity and shifted disagreement.}
Independent centered Gaussian snapshots at $t\in\{0.25,0.5,1\}$, with
$N=400$ per time and $500$ replications, are evaluated along the admissible
ridge~\eqref{eq:scalar-ridge}. Population means and variances agree to machine
precision, and the sampled centered excess negative log likelihood is exactly
zero: every candidate density is the same (Figure~\ref{fig:ambiguity}a). An
independent physically shifted population with initial mean $0.8$ is not
recentered data. On that holdout the candidates disagree, with Monte Carlo
excess loss matching the population Gaussian KL and final-time pairwise
$W_2$ reaching $0.329$ (Figure~\ref{fig:ambiguity}b). This is exact
observational equivalence on one population, not an estimator failure.

\paragraph{Two weak-identification mechanisms.}
Mean alternatives vary $a$ at fixed $(k,\beta)$; variance alternatives vary
$k$ at fixed $q$. Independent Gaussian snapshots at $t\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ and
$1000$ replications collapse across $N\in\{100,400,1600\}$ when plotted against
nuisance-adjusted information (Figure~\ref{fig:weak}). Exact two-point KL is
kept separate from efficient information. At the weakest displayed signals the
direct plug-in is invalid on $89.6\%$ of mean fits and $93.3\%$ of variance
fits. Unconditional bounded losses remain large there. The ratio of mean
information to $N\mu_0^2$ is constant at $0.237$ for this design; the ratio of
variance information to $N(\Sigma_0-q)^2$ ranges from $0.0140$ to $0.0528$, so
the latter is only a candidate scaling. These are information-theoretic
statements. They are not proved by the failure of a clipped ratio.
Figure~\ref{fig:weak} records the corresponding panels.

\paragraph{Excitation geometry and snapshot design.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry} is an exact local expansion, not a fitted
exponent. On times $\{0,0.5,1\}$ with $N=400$ per time, the ratio
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}/\mu_0^2$ is constant, and a log--log fit of
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ recovers slope $2.000$. Fitted slopes for
$\lambda_{\min}(I)$ are $1.96$ $(\mu_0\to 0)$ and $1.92$
$(\varepsilon\to 0)$, the $O(\mu_0^4)$ and $C(\varepsilon)-C(0)$ corrections
visible on a finite grid (Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}a,b). RMSE of $\hat a$
tracks mean excitation and stays small as $\varepsilon\to 0$
(Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}c), as required by
Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry}(iv): the lost parameter is $k$, not $a$. The phase diagram at this fixed budget is singular on both axes
$\mu_0=0$ and $\Sigma_0=q$, and recovery of $(a,b,\beta)$ rises only when
both excitations are large (Figure~\ref{fig:phase}).

Snapshot timing is non-monotonic. For two snapshots at $0$ and $\Delta$, with
moving variance, $I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ peaks at $\Delta\approx 1.41$ (value
$69.0$), against $1.25$ at $\Delta=0.12$. The frozen-variance minimizer of
$v_a(\Delta)$ is $\Delta^\star=1.109$, matching Corollary~\ref{cor:spacing}.
At equal total budget $N_{\mathrm{tot}}=1200$, an equal three-time allocation
has $\lambda_{\min}(I)=6.99$, while a two-time design raises
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ from $60.8$ to $87.3$ but drops $\lambda_{\min}$ to
$0.031$ because $I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}\approx 0$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-design}). More information about one parameter can
mean less identifiability of the model. Finite-grid plots of the expansions
are Figures~\ref{fig:scaling}--\ref{fig:design}.

\paragraph{Equal-budget recovery after shifted training.}
Three designs receive the same total number of observations at every time:
centered-only (mean $0$), shifted-only (mean $0.8$), and mixed (means
$-0.8,0,0.8$). Initial means are known experimental inputs; the common
$\Sigma_0$ is unknown and profiled in every arm. Evaluation uses one
independent test population with mean $1.2$, excluded from every fit. The
scientific constraints are $a>0$, $k\ge a$, and $q>0$. Compact optimizer
limits are recorded separately from genuine contacts with $b=0$.

The mixed design has two thirds of the shifted-only mean-block information
about $a$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:intervention}(iii)) and is not presumed to
win. The centered fit is a likelihood set along $a\in(0,\hat k]$, profiled
over the fitted $(k,q,\Sigma_0)$. The displayed envelope uses the compact
grid $[0.05,\min(\hat k,2.5)]$, so it is not automatically the full
scientific ridge, and mixes ridge variation with Monte Carlo uncertainty in
$(k,q,\Sigma_0)$. Test data are never used to pick a ridge member.

With $180$ replications and $4800$ samples per time, the centered forecast
envelope of exact Gaussian $W_2$ against the true test law remains
$(0.008,0.560)$ in the time-averaged mean and reaches $0.846$ at the last test
time. Shifted-only recovers $a$ with RMSE $0.023$ and mean $W_2$ $0.011$; mixed
has RMSE $0.029$ and mean $W_2$ $0.013$ (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}). Sampled
held-out NLL is reported separately from that population $W_2$. Profile
likelihood sets for $a$ after shifted or mixed training have coverage
$0.972$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,0.012$) and $0.961$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,0.014$) at this
budget, with mean hull widths $0.038$ and $0.058$. Those coverages are
compatible with a nominal $95\%$ level and are not declared calibrated from a
single cell. The comparison is an excitation and allocation tradeoff, not a
claim that mixing populations is automatically better.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Centered snapshots can identify a population law while leaving the
confinement--interaction split unresolved. That is an exact gauge, not a
finite-sample artifact. Near the gauge, identification is controlled by two
independent excitation directions, and the corresponding Fisher information
vanishes quadratically (Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry}). A small mean
destroys information about $a$; a variance near equilibrium destroys
information about $k$, hence about $b$ and $\beta$. Snapshot timing is
non-monotonic, and a design that maximizes information for one parameter can
erase identifiability of the model (Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-design}).
Physically shifted populations make the same ridge visible in forecasts, and
putting those shifted populations into training restores point recovery of
$a$ under equal budgets. Mixing several means is not automatically better
than a single shifted population: with known means and a common variance path
the mean-block information is proportional to $\sum_r N_r\mu_{0,r}^2$.

The numerical program is built to keep those statements from collapsing.
Centered fits are reported as sets. Forecast error on a shifted test
population is split into exact Gaussian $W_2$ and sampled NLL. Profile and
bootstrap intervals are diagnosed by regime; they are not declared calibrated
when coverage is produced by full-domain sets or by under-dispersed
resampling. Alignment remains a local interpretability check after the time
scale is fixed.

The results use quadratic $V$ and $W$, independent snapshots, and one
dimension. Recovery guarantees are for that scalar model. The general transfer
$H$ is a sufficient ambiguity under admissibility, well-posedness, uniqueness,
and $Hm_t=0$; it is not a classification of all interacting-energy gauges. A
shifted initialization reveals this gauge. It does not identify arbitrary
nonlinear $V$ and $W$. Whether a general convex interaction is identified by
the motion of its mean, or requires a richer excitation of the law, is open.
Whether an observed cross-sectional distribution
is usefully described by descent of~\eqref{eq:F} is open. A general
market-making game is not this gradient flow.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The closed form~\eqref{eq:affine} is special to quadratic confinement and
interaction. Interval constructions use compact numerical bounds that are not
scientific parameter boundaries. The minimax bound is for independent Gaussian
snapshots; particle systems from a single interacting cloud have a different
asymptotic covariance. Forward-solver comparisons, including quantile JKO, are
appendix checks of structure preservation. An official JKOnet* linear solver
run is reported in the appendix; its pairing, test split, and single-cloud
mixture encoding are not the exact-likelihood intervention experiment, so it
is not used as a recovery baseline.

\section{Background}
\label{app:background}

\paragraph{Dynamic optimal transport.}
By~\citet{BenamouBrenier00},
\[
\Wtwo^2(\mu,\nu)
=
\inf\Bigl\{
\int_0^1\int |v_t|^2\rho_t\,dx\,dt:
\partial_t\rho_t+\nabla\cdot(\rho_t v_t)=0,\ 
\rho_0=\mu,\ \rho_1=\nu
\Bigr\}.
\]
This is Otto's formal Riemannian structure~\cite{Otto01}: tangent vectors are
velocity potentials with $\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\phi)$ and metric
$\int\nabla\phi_1\cdot\nabla\phi_2\,\rho\,dx$. Equation~\eqref{eq:gf} is steepest
descent of $F$ in that metric.

\paragraph{Brenier maps.}
If $\mu$ is absolutely continuous, the optimal coupling of $\mu$ and $\nu$ is
induced by a $\mu$-a.e.\ unique map $T=\nabla\phi$ with $\phi$
convex~\cite{Brenier91,Villani03}. For the JKO step, the forward map from
$\rho_k$ to $\rho_{k+1}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:brenier-disc}
T_k(x)
=
x-\tau\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\rho}(\rho_{k+1})\bigl(T_k(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Evaluating the gradient at $x$ instead of at $T_k(x)$ changes the right-hand
side by $o(\tau)$ when the first variation is Lipschitz. In one dimension, $T$
is the monotone rearrangement of the two marginals, which is the map used in
the alignment statistic.

\paragraph{Mean-field games.}
A representative agent controls $dX_t=\alpha_t\,dt+\sqrt{2\nu}\,dB_t$. In
equilibrium,
\[
-\partial_t u-\nu\Delta u+H(x,\nabla u)=f(x,m),
\qquad
\partial_t m-\nu\Delta m-\nabla\cdot\bigl(m\,D_p H(x,\nabla u)\bigr)=0,
\]
with $m(0)=m_0$ and $u(T)=g(x,m(T))$~\cite{LasryLions07,CardaliaguetNotes}.
The Hamiltonian in this display does not depend on $m$; the coupling sits in
$f$ and $g$. The game is potential when $f=\delta G/\delta m$ and the terminal
cost is a variation of the same kind.

\begin{remark}[Myopic gradient flow versus a market-making game]
\label{rem:myopic}
That variational structure does not force the forward drift to equal
$-\nabla\delta F/\delta m$. A finite-horizon mean-field game has a backward
Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation whose spatial gradient determines the
equilibrium control. The autonomous Wasserstein gradient flow~\eqref{eq:gf}
is instead a myopic descent of a free energy, with no terminal condition and
no continuation value. Reading $x$ as signed inventory is a coordinate
convention, not an empirical claim about dealer data, and nothing in the
paper is a test of~\citet{AvellanedaStoikov08,GueantLehalleFernandez2013} on
observed inventories.
\end{remark}

\section{The one-dimensional JKO scheme}
\label{app:jko1d}

\begin{theorem}[Proximal map]
\label{thm:jko}
For every $\tau>0$ and every $\rho_0\in\Ptwo(\R)$ of finite energy, the
JKO step~\eqref{eq:jko} admits a unique minimizer. That minimizer is
absolutely continuous and positive almost everywhere, has unit mass, and
dissipates energy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:edp}
F(\rho_{k+1})+\frac1{2\tau}\Wtwo^2(\rho_{k+1},\rho_k)
\le
F(\rho_k).
\end{equation}
The piecewise-constant interpolants $\bar\rho_\tau$ converge to the unique
gradient flow of $F$ as $\tau\to 0$, uniformly on compact time intervals.
\end{theorem}

The optimal coupling is the monotone rearrangement, so
$\Wtwo^2(\rho,\rho_k)=\int_0^1(Q_\rho-Q_{\rho_k})^2\,du$ with $Q_\rho$ the
quantile function of $\rho$. Using $\rho(Q(u))=1/Q'(u)$ and writing $Q=Q_\rho$,
\[
F[Q]
=
\frac a2\int_0^1 Q^2\,du
+
\frac b2\Bigl(\int_0^1 Q^2-\Bigl(\int_0^1 Q\Bigr)^2\Bigr)
-
\beta\int_0^1\log Q'(u)\,du.
\]
Each JKO step minimizes $F[Q]+\frac1{2\tau}\int_0^1(Q-Q_k)^2\,du$ over
nondecreasing $Q$. Monotonicity is parametrized by strictly positive increments
on $M$ nodes and the program is solved by L-BFGS, with function tolerance
$10^{-12}$. Normalization and $Q'>0$ then hold by construction. An energy increment
is counted in the experiments only when it exceeds $10^{-9}$, so a reported
count of zero is a statement about the optimizer at that tolerance.
Theorem~\ref{thm:jko} is exact for the continuous proximal map.
In higher dimensions the rearrangement is unavailable; entropic proximal
steps~\cite{PeyreCuturi19} and neural parametrizations~\cite{JKOflow,Bunne22}
are the usual substitutes.

\section{Alignment as a post-identification check}
\label{app:alignment}

Between consecutive marginals, let $T$ be the monotone rearrangement,
$u_{\mathrm{obs}}=T-\mathrm{id}$, and $v^\theta$ the velocity~\eqref{eq:velocity}.
The alignment statistic is the cosine of $u_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $v^\theta$ in
$L^2(m_t)$. The vector $u_{\mathrm{obs}}$ displaces the two marginals. It is
the trajectory of a labeled agent only when identities are observed. In one
dimension the residual $1-\cos^2$ is unexplained displacement of that
rearrangement. It is not a rotational residual.

\begin{proposition}[Scale invariance]
\label{prop:blind}
For every $c>0$, $m^{c\theta}_t=m^\theta_{ct}$ and, at a fixed density,
$v^{c\theta}=c\,v^\theta$. Thus $\cos_{c\theta}=\cos_\theta$ for any observed
pair of marginals, while $\mu_{t+\Delta}/\mu_t=e^{-ca\Delta}$ whenever
$\mu_0\neq 0$.
\end{proposition}

The cosine is therefore an interpretability check after the time scale has been
fixed by the moment map~\eqref{eq:g}. It is a local comparison: $v^\theta$ is an
instantaneous tangent, while $T-\mathrm{id}$ is a finite displacement, so the
cosine moves with the window $\Delta$. The best-fit scale
$\hat\lambda=\langle u_{\mathrm{obs}},v^\theta\rangle/\|v^\theta\|^2$ absorbs
the window and, on the true flow, tracks $\Delta$. Numerical checks are in
Appendix~\ref{app:experiments}.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, $\mu(\rho)=\int x\,\rho$ and
$\Sigma(\rho)=\int x^2\rho-\mu(\rho)^2$.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge}}

The quadratic correction terms $\frac12\mathbb E[X^\top HX]$ and
$\frac14\mathbb E[(X-Y)^\top H(X-Y)]$ are finite for every
$\rho\in\Ptwo(\R^d)$. They do not make $F_{V,W,\beta}(\rho)$ real-valued when $V$
or $W$ has superquadratic growth, nor do they exclude $F=-\infty$. Restrict to
$\operatorname{dom} F$, so both energies lie in $\mathbb R$ and their difference
is defined. Let $X,Y$ be i.i.d.\ with law $\rho$, and write $m=\int x\,\rho$.
Because $H$ is symmetric,
\[
\mathbb E[(X-Y)^\top H(X-Y)]
=
2\mathbb E[X^\top HX]-2m^\top Hm.
\]
The potential correction contributes $\frac12\mathbb E[X^\top HX]$, while the
interaction correction contributes
$-\frac14\mathbb E[(X-Y)^\top H(X-Y)]$. Their sum is $\frac12 m^\top Hm$,
which is~\eqref{eq:gauge-energy}.

If $V$ and $W$ are differentiable, $\nabla V$ and $\nabla V_H$ exist
$\rho$-almost everywhere, and $\nabla W*\rho$, $\nabla W_H*\rho$ exist as
absolutely convergent integrals $\rho$-almost everywhere, the same identities
may be differentiated:
\[
\nabla\Bigl(\tfrac12 x^\top Hx\Bigr)=Hx,
\qquad
\nabla\Bigl(-\tfrac12 |\cdot|_H^2\Bigr)\ast\rho=-H(x-m),
\]
so the force difference is $Hm$ at $\rho$-almost every point, which
is~\eqref{eq:gauge-force}. Local integrability of the first variations does
not produce those convolutions. If a solution
of the untransformed equation satisfies $Hm_t=0$ on $[0,T]$, the two
McKean--Vlasov operators agree along that path. Existence, uniqueness, and
admissibility of both energies then identify the paths, hence all marginals.
The argument does not classify other gauges, and it does not remove the
requirement that both transformed components remain in the declared class.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gf}}

For $W(z)=\frac b2 z^2$,
$(W\ast\rho)(x)=\frac b2\bigl(x^2-2x\mu(\rho)+\int y^2\rho\bigr)$, so
$\nabla(W\ast\rho)=b(x-\mu)$ and $\nabla V(x)=ax$. Hence
\[
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\rho}
=
kx-b\mu(\rho)+\beta\,\nabla\log\rho.
\]
Substitution into~\eqref{eq:gf} gives~\eqref{eq:mv}. The continuity-equation
velocity is minus that gradient, which is~\eqref{eq:velocity}.

If $m_\infty$ is absolutely continuous with finite energy and
$\nabla\cdot\bigl(m_\infty\nabla\delta F/\delta\rho(m_\infty)\bigr)=0$,
testing against the first variation and integrating by parts yields
$\nabla\delta F/\delta\rho=0$ at $m_\infty$-almost every point, so the first
variation is constant on the support. Solving
$V+W\ast m_\infty+\beta(\log m_\infty+1)=\mathrm{const}$ gives the Gibbs form.
A constant first variation makes the velocity vanish. The argument does not
classify every weak stationary measure of finite second moment.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss}}

\emph{(ii).}
The mean $\mu_t$ is deterministic, so~\eqref{eq:mv} is the forward equation of
$dX_t=(-kX_t+b\mu_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2\beta}\,dB_t$. Taking expectations gives
$\dot\mu_t=-a\mu_t$, hence $\mu_t=\mu_0 e^{-at}$. Variation of constants gives
\[
X_t
=
e^{-kt}X_0
+
b\mu_0\int_0^t e^{-k(t-s)}e^{-as}\,ds
+
\sqrt{2\beta}\int_0^t e^{-k(t-s)}\,dB_s,
\]
and $b\int_0^t e^{-k(t-s)-as}\,ds=e^{-at}-e^{-kt}$, including the case $b=0$.
The stochastic integral is centered Gaussian with variance
$\frac{\beta}{k}(1-e^{-2kt})$, independent of $X_0$, which is~\eqref{eq:affine}.
The variance law follows by computing $\Var(X_t)$. The equation has a unique
solution in $C([0,\infty);\Ptwo)$, so this law is the gradient flow.
If $m_0$ has a finite cumulant of order $j\ge 3$, the Gaussian convolution
does not change that cumulant, and scaling $X_0-\mu_0$ by $e^{-kt}$ multiplies
it by $e^{-jkt}$.

\emph{(i).}
Sending $t\to\infty$ in~\eqref{eq:affine} yields $N(0,\sigma_\infty^2)$ from
every $m_0$. The same density is the Gibbs fixed point: the effective potential
$\frac k2 x^2-b\mu_\infty x$ is quadratic, and self-consistency forces
$\mu_\infty=0$ because $a>0$.

\emph{(iii).}
By McCann's criterion~\cite{McCann97}, $\beta\int\rho\log\rho$ is geodesically
convex, $\int V\rho$ is $a$-convex because $V''=a$, and the interaction energy
of a convex kernel is geodesically convex. Thus $F$ is $a$-convex. Contraction
of the flow and of the JKO resolvent follows from~\cite[Thm.~11.2.1 and
Thm.~4.0.4]{AGS08}. Since $\Wtwo(m_t,m_\infty)\ge|\mu_t|=e^{-at}|\mu_0|$, the
rate $a$ is sharp whenever $\mu_0\neq 0$, and it does not improve when $b$
increases. If $\mu_0=0$ the same $a$-convexity bound remains valid, but it is
no longer witnessed by the mean.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:jko}}

The Boltzmann entropy is infinite on measures that are not absolutely
continuous, while the proximal objective is finite at $\rho_k$, so every
minimizer is absolutely continuous. The entropy is bounded from below by the
second moment, $V$ and $W$ are nonnegative, and
$\frac1{2\tau}\Wtwo^2(\cdot,\rho_k)$ has tight sublevel sets. A minimizer
therefore exists by the direct method~\cite{AGS08}. The functional $F$ is
$a$-convex and $\frac1{2\tau}\Wtwo^2(\cdot,\rho_k)$ is $\frac1\tau$-convex along
generalized geodesics with base $\rho_k$, so the objective is strictly convex
along those curves and the minimizer is unique~\cite[Ch.~4]{AGS08}.

The Euler--Lagrange equation of the proximal step reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:el}
\log\rho_{k+1}
+
\beta^{-1}\bigl(V+W\ast\rho_{k+1}+\phi_k/\tau\bigr)
=
\mathrm{const}
\end{equation}
almost everywhere, where $\phi_k$ is a Kantorovich potential for the quadratic
cost from $\rho_{k+1}$ back to $\rho_k$, normalized so that the backward map is
$\mathrm{id}-\nabla\phi_k$~\cite{JKO98,Santambrogio15}. The logarithmic entropy
forces this density to be positive almost everywhere.

Mass and nonnegativity hold because the constraint set is $\Ptwo(\R)$.
Comparing the objective at $\rho_{k+1}$ with its value at $\rho_k$ gives
\eqref{eq:edp}. Summing it yields
$\sum_k\Wtwo^2(\rho_{k+1},\rho_k)\le 2\tau\bigl(F(\rho_0)-\inf F\bigr)$, and the
piecewise-constant interpolants satisfy
$\Wtwo(\bar\rho_\tau(t),\bar\rho_\tau(s))\le C\sqrt{|t-s|+\tau}$.
They are discontinuous, so they are not elements of $C([0,T];\Ptwo)$. The
refined Ascoli theorem in $(\Ptwo,\Wtwo)$ produces a subsequence converging
uniformly on compact time intervals to a continuous curve, which $a$-convexity
identifies as the unique gradient flow~\cite[Thm.~11.1.6 and
Thm.~4.0.4]{AGS08}. Uniqueness of the limit gives convergence of the whole
family.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}}

If $\mu_0=0$, then~\eqref{eq:affine} depends on $\theta$ only through $(k,\beta)$.
If $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\sigma_\infty^2)$, the scaled initial deviation and the
added Gaussian recombine into $N(0,\sigma_\infty^2)$, so
$X_t\sim N(\mu_0 e^{-at},\sigma_\infty^2)$.

For (iii), $\mu_1/\mu_0=e^{-a\Delta}$ determines $a$ when $\mu_0\neq 0$. The
variance increments are
$\Sigma_{j+1}-\Sigma_j=(\Sigma_0-\sigma_\infty^2)e^{-2kj\Delta}(e^{-2k\Delta}-1)$.
They are nonzero for $j=0,1$ when $\Sigma_0\neq\sigma_\infty^2$, so their ratio
equals $e^{-2k\Delta}$ and determines $k$. Then
$\Sigma_1-\Sigma_0=(\Sigma_0-\sigma_\infty^2)(r-1)$ determines
$\sigma_\infty^2$, and $b=k-a$, $\beta=k\sigma_\infty^2$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-mean}}

The alternatives $(a,k,\beta)$ and $(a+\delta,k,\beta)$ have the same variance
path $S_j$. Independent Gaussian snapshots therefore differ only in their
means. The KL between $N(\mu_0 e^{-at_j},S_j)$ and
$N(\mu_0 e^{-(a+\delta)t_j},S_j)$, summed over $N_j$ observations, is
\eqref{eq:mean-kl}. If $\mu_0$ is unknown, the alternative mean that minimizes
that quadratic in the weights $w_j=N_j/S_j$ is the displayed weighted
projection, and the local expansion of the minimized KL is
$\delta^2 I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}/2+o(\delta^2\mu_0^2)$ with~\eqref{eq:mean-info}. Under
a fixed nondegenerate design the time sums are positive constants, so
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ scales as $N\mu_0^2$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-var}}

Holding $(a,\mu_0,\Sigma_0,q)$ fixed, the alternatives differ only in their
variance paths, and both are required to remain admissible. The KL between
Gaussians $N(\mu_0 e^{-at_j},S_j(k))$ and
$N(\mu_0 e^{-at_j},S_j(k+\delta))$ is~\eqref{eq:var-kl}. In the coordinates
$(\Sigma_0,k,q)$ the variance derivatives are $r$, $-2\varepsilon\, t r$, and
$1-r$. The Gaussian variance information inner product is
$\langle u,v\rangle_S$, whose weights $1/S_j^2$ depend on $\varepsilon$ through
$S_j=q+\varepsilon r_j$. The efficient score for $k$ is the residual of
$\partial_k S$ after $S$-orthogonal projection onto
$\operatorname{span}\{r,1-r\}$, which is~\eqref{eq:var-info}. Factoring
$\varepsilon$ from the residual produces
$I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon^2 C(\varepsilon;k,q,\{t_j,N_j\})$. As $\varepsilon\to 0$,
$S_j\to q$ uniformly on a finite time grid, so the inner product converges to
the constant-weight product at $S\equiv q$. If
$\{r,1-r,-2tr\}$ has rank three in that limit, the residual of $-2tr$ stays
bounded away from zero and $C(\varepsilon)\to C(0)>0$. Three distinct times with
positive $N_j$ suffice. Two distinct times make the variance tangent
two-dimensional, so the projection of any third direction onto
$\operatorname{span}\{r,1-r\}$ is exact and $I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=0$ even if
$\varepsilon\neq 0$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:lecam}}

Let $P_0,P_1$ be the two fully specified product experiments, with exact KL
$K$. Pinsker's inequality yields $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)\le\sqrt{K/2}$.
Le Cam's two-point method then bounds the maximum risk of any estimator of
the varied coordinate: the tests that try to decide which of two points
separated by $|\delta|$ is true have error at least
$\frac12\bigl(1-\mathrm{TV}\bigr)$, and the displayed quadratic-risk bound
follows by restricting the loss to the event that the estimator is at least
$|\delta|/2$ from the nearer point~\cite[Theorem~2.2]{Tsybakov09}.
If the alternative may use a different admissible initial mean, replace $K$
by the minimized KL. That value is no larger, so $1-\sqrt{K/2}$ is no smaller,
and the numerical lower bound can only increase.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:intervention}}

The affine representation~\eqref{eq:affine} with parameters $\theta_h$ is
\[
X_t^{(h)}
=
\mu_0 e^{-(a+h)t}
+
e^{-kt}(X_0-\mu_0)
+
\sqrt{q(1-e^{-2kt})}\,Z.
\]
If $\mu_0=0$, the law does not depend on $h$. If $X_0=s+Y_0$ with $Y_0$
centered, the two laws at a fixed $t$ are translations of a common centered
law, so their $W_2$ distance is the absolute mean gap, which is~(ii). For
known design means and a common variance path, the Gaussian mean-block
information for $a$ is additive across independent populations and equals
\[
\sum_r\mu_{0,r}^2\sum_j\frac{N_{r,j}t_j^2 e^{-2at_j}}{S_j}.
\]
With identical times, allocations per time, and variance paths, this factors
as $\bigl(\sum_r N_r\mu_{0,r}^2\bigr)$ times a common time sum, which is~(iii).
Under the stated mixed versus shifted-only allocation the ratio of those
mean-square excitations is $2/3$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:est}}

By~\eqref{eq:affine}, each $m_{t_j}$ has a finite fourth moment. Let $\hat\eta$
be the vector of sample means and unbiased sample variances at the three times,
and let $\eta$ be its expectation. The strong law gives $\hat\eta\to\eta$ almost
surely. Independence of the snapshots and the central limit theorem give
$\sqrt N(\hat\eta-\eta)\Rightarrow N(0,\Gamma)$, with the stated blocks: the
covariance of $X$ and $(X-\mu)^2$ is the third cumulant, and
$\Var((X-\mu)^2)=\kappa_4+2\Sigma^2$~\cite[Ch.~3]{vanderVaart98}.
The map $g$ in~\eqref{eq:g} is $C^1$ on
$\{\mu_1/\mu_0>0,\ \Sigma_1\neq\Sigma_0,\ r\in(0,1)\}$, and $\eta$ lies in that
set by Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}(iii). Thus
$\Pr(\hat\eta\in U)\to 1$, $g(\hat\eta)\to\theta$ almost surely, and the delta
method gives the limiting normal law with Jacobian $J=\nabla g(\eta)$.

For~\eqref{eq:avar}, $\hat a=-\Delta^{-1}\log(\hat\mu_1/\hat\mu_0)$ depends only
on two independent sample means, with variances $\Sigma_0/N$ and $\Sigma_1/N$.
The gradient in $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ is $\Delta^{-1}(1/\mu_0,-1/\mu_1)$, and
$\mu_1=\mu_0 e^{-a\Delta}$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lam}}

Snapshot $j$ consists of $N$ i.i.d.\ draws from
$N(\mu_j,\Sigma_j)$, with $\mu_j=\mu_0 e^{-at_j}$ and $\Sigma_j$ as
in~\eqref{eq:moments}. The model is differentiable in quadratic mean, so the
product experiment is locally asymptotically normal with information
$N\sum_j I_j(\vartheta)$~\cite[Ch.~7]{vanderVaart98}. For a univariate normal,
the information in the mean and variance parameters is
$\mathrm{diag}(1/v,\ 1/(2v^2))$. The chain rule gives
\[
I_j
=
\frac{\nabla\mu_j\nabla\mu_j^\top}{\Sigma_j}
+
\frac{\nabla\Sigma_j\nabla\Sigma_j^\top}{2\Sigma_j^2}.
\]
The mean depends only on $(\mu_0,a)$ and the variance only on
$(\Sigma_0,a+b,\sigma_\infty^2)$, so the two blocks do not interact.

On the mean block,
$\nabla_{(\mu_0,a)}\mu_j=\mu_j(1/\mu_0,-t_j)$.
The information equals $\sum_j\omega_j(1/\mu_0,-t_j)^{\otimes 2}$. Its Schur
complement in the $a$-coordinate is $\sum_j\omega_j\Var_\omega(t)$, which is
positive when $\mu_0\neq 0$ and at least two distinct times are observed.
The local asymptotic minimax theorem~\cite[Thm.~8.11]{vanderVaart98}, applied
to the submodel with variance parameters known, gives the stated bound at an
interior point $b>0$. The
supremum over local alternatives in the full model is at least as large.

Weighted least squares with weights $N/\hat\Sigma_j$ is a $\sqrt N$-consistent
M-estimator with consistent weights. Its asymptotic covariance is the inverse
of $\sum_j\omega_j(1/\mu_0,-t_j)^{\otimes 2}$~\cite[Ch.~5]{vanderVaart98}, so it
attains the bound. For two snapshots the model in the two means is just
identified, least squares reproduces $\hat a$, and
$\sum_j\omega_j\Var_\omega(t)=\Delta^2\omega_0\omega_1/(\omega_0+\omega_1)$
inverts to $v_a(\Delta)$.

On the variance block with three snapshots and $\Sigma_0\neq\sigma_\infty^2$,
the map $(\Sigma_0,a+b,\sigma_\infty^2)\mapsto(\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2)$ is a
local diffeomorphism by the same algebra as Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}(iii).
For Gaussian samples the sample variances are asymptotically efficient, with
variance $2\Sigma_j^2$ for the $\sqrt N$-scaled error, and efficiency is
preserved by a smooth reparametrization. Block diagonality means that unknown
mean parameters do not inflate this limit. Therefore the plug-in estimators of
$a+b$ and $\sigma_\infty^2$ are efficient~\cite[Thm.~8.8]{vanderVaart98}, and so
is $\beta=(a+b)\sigma_\infty^2$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:spacing}}

If $\Sigma_1=\Sigma_0$, then
$v_a(\Delta)=\frac{\Sigma_0}{\mu_0^2}a^2 f(a\Delta)$ with
$f(x)=(1+e^{2x})/x^2$. The function $f$ tends to infinity at $0$ and at
infinity, and
$f'(x)=2x^{-3}(xe^{2x}-1-e^{2x})$ vanishes if and only if $x=1+e^{-2x}$.
The two sides of that equation are strictly monotone in opposite directions, so
there is a unique positive root. It is the global minimizer, and
$x^\star\approx 1.109$.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry}}

Mean and variance of a Gaussian snapshot are independent, the mean path
depends on $(a,\mu_0)$ only, and the variance path $(S_j)$ does not depend on
$\mu_0$. The mean score for $a$ is $\partial_a\mu_j=-t_j\mu_0 e^{-at_j}$, hence
linear in $\mu_0$. Profiling $\mu_0$ is an orthogonal projection in the
weighted $\ell^2$ space with weights $N_j/S_j$. Those weights are independent
of $\mu_0$, so the projected score remains linear in $\mu_0$ and
$I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}=\mu_0^2 I_a^{(1)}$ with $I_a^{(1)}$ equal
to~\eqref{eq:mean-info} at $\mu_0=1$. The factor is positive whenever the
weighted observation times are not all equal, i.e.\ whenever at least two
distinct times have positive allocation. This is~(i). Claim~(ii) is
Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-var}.

For~(iii), write $\theta=(a,b,\beta)$. After projecting out
$(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$, the Fisher matrix splits as
$I(\mu_0,\varepsilon)=I_{\mathrm{var}}(\varepsilon)+\mu_0^2 B$, where $B$ is
the mean-block contribution in $(a,b,\beta)$ coordinates. By~(i), $B$ does
not depend on $\mu_0$. The mean path is independent of $(b,\beta)$, so $B$
is supported on the $a$-coordinate and $B_{aa}=I_a^{(1)}$.

Variance statistics identify $(k,q)$ after $\Sigma_0$ is profiled. The map
$(a,b,\beta)\mapsto(k,q)=(a+b,\beta/(a+b))$ has kernel spanned by
$(1,-1,0)$. Thus, whenever $\varepsilon\neq 0$ and three distinct times make
$(k,q)$ locally identified, $I_{\mathrm{var}}(\varepsilon)$ has a simple
kernel along the unit gauge $u=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt{2}$. Along that direction,
$u^\top B u=I_a^{(1)}/2$. Ordinary perturbation of a simple eigenvalue of a
symmetric matrix then yields
\[
\lambda_{\min}\bigl(I(\mu_0,\varepsilon)\bigr)
=
\frac{\mu_0^2}{2}I_a^{(1)}+O(\mu_0^4)
\qquad(\mu_0\to 0),
\]
and the associated eigenspace approaches $u$.

At fixed $\mu_0\neq 0$, the mean block locally identifies $a$. As
$\varepsilon\to 0$, Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-var} gives
$I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=\varepsilon^2 C(\varepsilon)=\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ under
three distinct times. The remaining null of the variance block at
$\varepsilon=0$ is the $k$-direction at fixed $q$, namely $(0,1,q)$ in
$(a,b,\beta)$. Hence $\lambda_{\min}=\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$ and the associated
direction approaches $(0,1,q)$. Claim~(iv) is the combination of~(i)--(iii).\qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-design}}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry}(ii), any two-time experiment with unknown
$(\Sigma_0,q)$ has $I_k^{\mathrm{eff}}=0$ for every $\varepsilon$. The
profiled matrix $I(\mu_0,\varepsilon)$ is then singular, so
$\lambda_{\min}(I)=0$ even if $I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is large. A two-time
allocation that maximizes $I_a^{\mathrm{eff}}$ therefore need not maximize
$\lambda_{\min}$. An informative design must occupy an interior time scale
and be scored jointly.\qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}}

Replacing $\theta$ by $c\theta$ multiplies the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:mv}
by $c$. Thus $t\mapsto m^\theta_{ct}$ solves the equation for $c\theta$ with the
same initial datum, and uniqueness gives $m^{c\theta}_t=m^\theta_{ct}$. At a fixed
density the velocity~\eqref{eq:velocity} is linear in $\theta$, so
$v^{c\theta}=c\,v^\theta$, and the cosine is invariant under positive scaling of
either argument. The mean ratio is~\eqref{eq:moments}.\qed

\paragraph{Solver and particle checks.}
These remaining checks use the implicit conservative scheme, or one cloud of
interacting particles, as indicated. They are numerical verification
of~\eqref{eq:mv} and of the diagnostics. They are not independent-snapshot
replications of Theorems~\ref{thm:est} and~\ref{thm:lam}.

\paragraph{Quantile JKO.}
In one dimension, $\Wtwo^2(\rho,\rho_k)=\int_0^1(Q-Q_k)^2\,du$ for the quantile
function $Q$, and each JKO step is a strictly convex program over nondecreasing
$Q$ (Appendix~\ref{app:jko1d}), solved by L-BFGS. We compare it with explicit
and implicit conservative schemes on $[-6,6]$. Above the diffusive CFL limit the
explicit scheme loses positivity and diverges. Implicit Euler and quantile JKO
remain stable. On stable runs, energy increments larger than $10^{-9}$ do not
occur. Signed mass of a stable Eulerian scheme agrees with $1$ to about
$10^{-15}$; quantile JKO is normalized by construction.

For accuracy we start from $N(1,1)$, so the law remains
$N(\mu_T,\Sigma_T)$, and we measure $W_2$ at $T=1$
(Table~\ref{tab:conv}, Figure~\ref{fig:conv}). A log--log fit on
$\tau\in\{0.2,0.1,0.05,0.025,0.0125\}$ gives slope $0.95$ at $M=240$ quantiles
and $0.96$ at $M=480$. At fixed $\tau=0.05$ the error barely changes from
$M=60$ to $M=480$. JKO at $M=480$, $\tau=0.0125$ reaches $W_2=0.0028$ in
$0.39\,\mathrm{s}$. The finest implicit run in the table reaches $0.018$ in

\paragraph{Relaxation.}
From $m_0=\tfrac12 N(-2,0.2^2)+\tfrac12 N(2,0.2^2)$, the implicit scheme with
$\Delta t=2\times 10^{-3}$ and $481$ nodes contracts onto $N(0,1/3)$. The energy
has no increments above $10^{-9}$, the terminal variance is $0.345$ against
$1/3$, and $\Wtwo(\cdot,m_\infty)$ falls from $1.55$ to $0.010$
(Figure~\ref{fig:exp01}). The excess variance is numerical diffusion. The
shifted mixture $\tfrac12 N(-1.2,0.2^2)+\tfrac12 N(2.8,0.2^2)$ has mean-law
RMSE $0.0019$ against $\mu_0 e^{-at}$.

\paragraph{Liquidity shock.}
With $c_t=2$ on $[2,4)$ and $c_t=0$ otherwise, the exact mean at $t=4$ is
$2(1-e^{-2})\approx 1.729$. The implicit scheme reaches $1.720$, with path RMSE
$0.006$. A sweep over $c\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $\Delta T\in\{0.5,1,2\}$ has mean
absolute error $0.009$ against $c(1-e^{-a\Delta T})$ (Figure~\ref{fig:exp02}).
The untilted energy increases on every forced step and on none after the tilt
returns to zero. The tilted energy jumps when $c$ switches, because the
functional changes, and then decreases on each constant-$c$ interval.

\paragraph{Comparative statics.}
Equilibrium variance decreases in $b$ and tracks $\beta/(a+b)$, with maximum
absolute error $0.021$ on the $b$-sweep. Fitted mean rates recover $a$ with
mean absolute error $0.029$ (Figure~\ref{fig:exp03}).

\paragraph{Solver structure.}
Below the diffusive CFL restriction $\Delta t\le(\Delta x)^2/(2\beta)$, explicit
Euler conserves signed mass to about $10^{-15}$ and stays nonnegative on this
test. Above CFL it loses positivity, and the signed integral then blows up with
the instability. Implicit Euler remains stable above CFL, conserves signed mass
to about $10^{-15}$, and stayed nonnegative on this test. Quantile JKO has no
CFL restriction (Table~\ref{tab:solvers}, Figure~\ref{fig:exp04}).

\paragraph{Interacting particles.}

\paragraph{Variance-interval diagnosis.}
Profile sets are stored as components, including full-domain sets; an interval
hull is labeled as a hull. Weak-signal profile sets cover because they are
often the whole compact domain. With $180$ replications, binomial standard
errors are about $0.02$ near coverage $0.93$. At the strongest displayed cell
($N=1600$, scaled excitation $16$), the full-domain rate is $0$, nominal
$95\%$ profile coverage is $0.933$ ($\mathrm{SE}\,0.019$), compatible with
$0.95$, and the mean hull width remains $1.27$ on a domain of width $1.99$.
The bootstrap undercovers throughout, with coverage from $0.05$ to $0.775$.
Clipped oracle Wald coverage ranges from $0.906$ to $1$
(Figure~\ref{fig:intervals}).

\paragraph{Alignment against moment restrictions.}
The predicted velocity uses a Gaussian-kernel score with Silverman's bandwidth.
On one fixed particle trajectory, replacing $(a,b,\beta)$ by
$c(a,b,\beta)$ for $c\in\{1/2,1,2\}$ leaves the mean cosine unchanged to
numerical precision, as Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} requires. A separate,
nonuniform parameter error, $(1,0.5,0.5)\mapsto(2,2,0.2)$, moves the mean
cosine from $0.88$ to $0.81$ while the next-step variance error jumps from
$0.019$ to $0.38$. A quartic perturbation
$V=\frac a2 x^2+\frac{0.3}{4}x^4$ produces a higher cosine than the true
quadratic flow, $0.91$ against $0.88$, while the variance error rises from
$0.019$ to $0.090$ (Figure~\ref{fig:falsify}). Changing the kernel bandwidth by
factors $1/2$, $1$, and $2$ moves the true-flow cosine through
$0.923$, $0.872$, and $0.813$. The cosine also falls with the window, from
$0.87$ at $\Delta=0.05$ to $0.35$ at $\Delta=0.40$. The fitted scale tracks the
window: $\hat\lambda\approx 0.020$ at $\Delta=0.02$ and $\hat\lambda\approx 0.048$
at $\Delta=0.05$. Alignment is not a substitute for the moment map or for the
intervention experiments.

\paragraph{Official JKOnet* attempt.}
We pinned the public JKOnet* code of~\citet{Terpin24} at revision
\texttt{1741c53} and ran the released \texttt{jkonet-star-linear} solver on
independent snapshots of the same quadratic model, preserving its coupling,
GMM-density, and linear least-squares pipeline. Consecutive snapshot labels
are treated as unit steps. For physical spacing $\Delta$, the official unit
clock therefore corresponds to continuous coefficients $\Delta(a,b,\beta)$
rather than $(a,b,\beta)$; that global time rescaling is separate from the
finite-step JKO approximation of the flow. The attempt uses equally spaced
times $\{0,0.5,1\}$ rather than the unequal grid of Experiments~12 and~14,
so $\Delta=1/2$ and a faithful coefficient comparison would rescale by that
factor. The polynomial degree is set to $2$ inside the official linear
objective. On a
quick equal-budget check with $300$ samples per time, the official one-step
$W_1$ error is $0.078$ after centered training and $0.073$ after shifted-only
training. Concatenating the mixed means into one unlabeled cloud produces a
mean-zero mixture, which is not the three-population design of
Experiment~14, and the official test split is particles from the same
populations rather than an independent shifted intervention. The released CLI has no population identifier, so a faithful mixed-arm
comparison would require a population-aware adapter around the official linear
normal equations rather than pooling clouds before OT. Those gaps are
recorded in \texttt{results/exp16\_jkonet\_star\_comparison.json}. The run is
therefore a verified execution of the official solver, not a substitute for
the exact-likelihood recovery experiment.
