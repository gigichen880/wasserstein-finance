# Claim-level novelty audit

Audited October 5, 2026. The comparison distinguishes exact marginal paths,
finite independent snapshots, inferred OT couplings, and labeled trajectories.
These observation models are not interchangeable.

| Work | Observation model | Unknowns | Main recovery statement | Diffusion | Potential--interaction ambiguity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bunne et al., **JKOnet** ([arXiv:2106.06345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06345)) | Finite unlabeled population point clouds; model-implied transport maps | A measure-linear potential energy represented by a neural potential | Algorithmic JKO learning and prediction; no uniqueness theorem for the energy | Not separately estimated | Not covered; interaction was outside the model |
| Terpin et al., **JKOnet*** ([arXiv:2406.12616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12616)) | Finite marginals/point clouds; OT couplings inferred between consecutive marginals | Potential, interaction, and entropy coefficient | Linear-dictionary least squares has a unique optimizer when the stacked feature Gram matrix is invertible | Unknown entropy coefficient is fitted | Not characterized; the proposed ambiguity should appear as rank failure or ill-conditioning |
| Persiianov et al., **iJKOnet** ([arXiv:2506.01502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01502)) | Independent samples from finite, temporally unpaired marginals, modeled as exact JKO steps | Practically potential, interaction, and entropy; theorem treats potential-only recovery | Objective-gap control for a pure potential under strict convexity/smoothness; interaction/internal guarantees left open | Learnable in implementation, not covered by recovery theorem | Not covered |
| Guan et al., **Gradient-Flow SDEs Have Unique Transient Population Dynamics** ([PMLR 300](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/guan26a.html)) | Exact marginal flow; three-marginal corollary uses random separated times and a countable candidate class | One fixed gradient drift and scalar diffusivity | Joint drift/diffusion identifiability iff the marginal flow is nonstationary | Unknown and jointly identified | Not applicable: there is one drift, not a potential/interaction decomposition |
| Nguyen and Malysheva, **Beyond Gradient Flow** ([arXiv:2609.35060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35060)) | Finite independent snapshots; separately analyzes stronger source information \((\rho_t,\partial_t\rho_t)\) | General drift field | Characterizes weighted-solenoidal drift gauges, source-rank conditions, finite-snapshot aliasing, and weak tangent directions | Known | Closest conceptual overlap, but its gauge is within one drift field rather than between shared potential and interaction components |
| Lang and Lu, **Identifiability of interaction kernels in mean-field equations** ([arXiv:2106.05565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05565)) | A weak population-density solution over a full time interval | Interaction kernel only | Identifiable function space is generated by nonzero modes of a data-dependent compact operator; full-space identification requires strict positivity | Fixed/known | No simultaneously unknown external potential |

## Closest-overlap conclusions

1. JKOnet* is the closest computational baseline because it fits potential,
   interaction, and entropy jointly. Its invertible-Gram condition assumes the
   needed excitation; it does not characterize when centered populations make
   the decomposition rank deficient.
2. Guan et al. identify a single transient gradient drift. In a centered
   quadratic interacting model that drift contains \(a+b\), so their result
   can identify the combined drift without separating \(a\) from \(b\).
3. Nguyen--Malysheva is the closest conceptual work on gauges, weak directions,
   and finite-snapshot aliasing. The distinct target here is a
   **component-decomposition gauge** under a common interacting energy,
   unknown entropy strength, and multiple physically initialized populations.
4. Lang--Lu provides the correct excitation/operator language for an unknown
   interaction kernel, but assumes the external potential is not another
   unknown component and uses a full density path rather than sparse empirical
   snapshots.

## Defensible novelty claims

Subject to a broader search before submission:

- We exhibit an explicit gauge that transfers quadratic structure between
  external confinement and pairwise interaction while leaving a population
  path unchanged when its mean lies in the gauge null space.
- The gauge persists on top of nonlinear base energies, producing identical
  non-Gaussian transients under suitable symmetry and well-posedness.
- In a finite independent-snapshot experiment, we quantify deterioration near
  the gauge separately for weak mean excitation and weak variance excitation.
- We show that models with identical centered-population laws can make
  consequentially different forecasts for a physically shifted population.
- Shared-energy initial-population interventions remove this particular gauge
  when their means are informative.

## Claims to avoid

- “First identifiability result from population snapshots.”
- “First method to fit potential, interaction, and diffusion.”
- “Three snapshots generally identify an interacting energy.”
- “Transience alone separates potential from interaction.”
- “JKOnet* proves all fitted energy components are structurally identifiable.”
- “Lang--Lu proves interaction recovery from sparse point-cloud snapshots.”
- “A shifted population establishes complete nonlinear identifiability.”
- “Recentring an existing dataset is an intervention.”
- Any unconditional “first” claim before a broader mean-field inverse-problem
  search.

## Planned baseline use

The checkpoint does not require a neural learner. If the three gate conditions
are met, the next stage should run the official JKOnet* linear-functional
implementation on a correctly specified quadratic dictionary:

1. centered training populations, where the feature Gram matrix should expose
   the ridge;
2. a physically shifted holdout, where fitted ridge points disagree;
3. shifted populations added to training, where the ridge should contract.

Any adaptation must be documented and cannot be described as an official
reproduction if it changes the observation pairing or objective.
